# Power BI — Report Page Specifications

6 report pages (+ 1 drill-through target). All pages share report-level
**slicers**: Date range (DimDate[date]), **RFM Segment** (DimCustomer[segment]),
**Region** (DimCountry[region]) and **Product Category** (DimProduct[category]).
Slicers are synced across pages; every visual is clickable for cross-filtering.

## Design language (applied to all pages)

| Element | Rule |
|---------|------|
| Canvas | 1280 × 800 (16:9), single screen, **no horizontal/vertical scrolling**, consistent 20px margins, 12px gaps |
| Theme | Dark navy background `#1B2A4A`, cards `#24345A`, gold accent `#F2C94C`, white text |
| Typography | Page title 21px/700 · subtitle 12px · section header 11px uppercase · KPI label 10px uppercase · KPI value 23px/700 · chart labels 10px |
| Currency | `£` thousands-separated, `0.00` for averages, whole pounds for totals; compact `£x.xm/£xxxk` on axes |
| Percentages | one decimal (`0.0%`); region shares must sum to 100% |
| Counts | integers with thousands separators |
| Brand | Header top-right: **RETAIL ANALYTICS · \<SECTION\>** (e.g. "RETAIL ANALYTICS · EXECUTIVE VIEW") |
| Insight | Every page has one compact "Key Insight" callout, **computed from data** (never hard-coded) |

Each page is reproduced 1:1 in `powerbi/previews/*.html` + `.png`, generated by
`powerbi/scripts/generate_preview.py` from the validated dataset. The previews
are pixel-checked (fixed 1280×800, zero element overflow).

## Page 1 — Retail Executive Overview

*Purpose: the 10-second answer to "how is the business doing?".*

| Area | Visual | Fields / Measure |
|------|--------|------------------|
| KPI row (6 cards) | Card | Total Revenue, Total Orders, Average Order Value, Total Units Sold, Total Customers, Repeat Customer Rate, UK Share |

> **Attribution note (never implied as total revenue):** the segment donut uses
> **Customer Revenue** (£8,887,208.89) — transactions without a CustomerID
> (134,658 line items) are excluded. The card title reads
> *"Customer-Attributed Revenue by RFM Segment"* with the subtitle
> *"Excludes transactions without CustomerID"*.

## Page 2 — Sales & Trends

*Purpose: revenue dynamics over the 13-month window (Dec 2010 – Dec 2011).*

| Area | Visual | Fields / Measure |
|------|--------|------------------|
| KPI row (4 cards) | Card | Peak Month, Slowest Month, Highest Revenue Day, Average Monthly Revenue |
| Monthly revenue | Line chart | X = DimDate[year_month_name]; Y = Total Revenue |
| Cumulative revenue | Line/area chart | X = DimDate[year_month_name]; Y = **full-window running total** |
| Weekday | Column chart | X = DimDate[day_of_week_name]; Y = Total Revenue |
| Insight | Text callout | Peaks/troughs + highest-revenue weekday, computed from data |

> **Axis labels:** use compact month labels with the year shown at the start of
> the series and at each year boundary (e.g. `Dec 10, Jan, Feb, … Dec 11`) so the
> 13 points never overlap.
>
> **Cumulative vs YTD:** the preview's *Cumulative Revenue* series is the running
> total across the **full window** (13 months). The model's `Running Revenue YTD`
> DAX measure is a genuine calendar **YTD** (`TOTALYTD`) and resets each January —
> the two are intentionally different; do not label the window-cumulative series
> "YTD".
>
> **Trading-day finding (genuine data result):** the source contains **no
> Saturday transactions** (0 trading days across Dec 2010–Dec 2011), so
> Saturday renders £0. Sunday is real trading: £806,790.78 across 50 Sundays.
> The subtitle documents this rather than hiding it. Do not impute Saturday data.

## Page 3 — Customer Intelligence

*Purpose: customer value and RFM behaviour. The strongest page — preserve structure.*

| Area | Visual | Fields / Measure |
|------|--------|------------------|
| KPI row (5 cards) | Card | Total Customers, Repeat Customer Rate, **Average Orders per Customer**, **Average Revenue per Customer**, Average Recency (days) |
| Segment table | Table | DimCustomer[segment]; Customers, Revenue, Avg Revenue / Customer, % of Base (subtle data bars) |
| Repeat mix | Donut | Legend = DimCustomer[is_repeat] (Repeat / One-time); Value = Total Customers |
| Segment revenue | Horizontal bar | Y = DimCustomer[segment]; X = Revenue + % of customer-attributed revenue |
| Insight | Text callout | Champions concentration (computed): "Champions are 17.8% of customers but generate 54.5% of customer-attributed revenue." |

> **Terminology:** use **AVERAGE REVENUE PER CUSTOMER** (= Total Revenue ÷ Total
> Customers, £2,448) and **AVERAGE ORDERS PER CUSTOMER** (5.1) — never "AVG
> customer value" / "avg orders/customer".
>
> RFM columns come from **DimCustomer**, generated with the exact validated SQL
> NTILE methodology — 100% reconciliation with the workbook and SQL layer.

## Page 4 — Product Performance

*Purpose: what sells, and what earns.*

| Area | Visual | Fields / Measure |
|------|--------|------------------|
| KPI row (4 cards) | Card | **Unique Products** (3,947), Top Product, **Average Unit Price** (£1.95), **Product Categories** (831) |
| Top products | Horizontal bar | Y = DimProduct[description] (top 10 by Product Revenue Rank); X = Total Revenue (+ %) |
| Category revenue | Horizontal bar | Y = DimProduct[category] (top 10); X = Total Revenue (+ %) |
| Revenue vs volume | Scatter | X = Units (log), Y = Revenue (log), Details = stock_code; median quadrant lines; top 120 products by revenue |
| Insight | Text callout | e.g. "The top 10 products drive 11.0% of revenue; the largest line is Dotcom Postage (1.9%)." |

> **Layout:** single 1280px-wide page — no horizontal scrolling; the scatter
> occupies the lower band so the page uses space with purpose.
>
> **Data-quality note:** `description` is blank for 114 of 3,947 stock codes
> (their `category` is blank — group as *"Uncategorised"* in the visual).
> `category` is the first word of the description (831 distinct values, e.g.
> SET, PINK, BLUE) — an **automatic grouping**, not a curated taxonomy. The KPI
> therefore reads "Product Categories" with the subtitle *"auto-grouped by first
> word"*.
>
> **Scatter quadrants** (median-split, genuine product archetypes):
> high revenue/high volume, high revenue/low volume (niche premium), low
> revenue/high volume (commodity), low revenue/low volume.

## Page 5 — Geographic Performance

*Purpose: where revenue comes from.*

| Area | Visual | Fields / Measure |
|------|--------|------------------|
| KPI row (5 cards) | Card | **Countries With Sales** (38), then region shares: **UK & Ireland 87.4%**, Europe 10.3%, Asia Pacific 2.0%, Other Markets 0.3% — **mutually exclusive, sum to 100.0%** |
| Region | Donut | Legend = DimCountry[region] (all 6 groups); Value = Total Revenue |
| Country metrics | Table | Top 10 countries by revenue: Country, Customers, Revenue, Average Revenue per Customer (ARPC), % of Total |
| Top countries | Horizontal bar | Y = DimCountry[country] (top 15); X = Total Revenue (+ %) |
| Insight | Text callout | e.g. "UK & Ireland generate 87.4% of revenue (the UK alone 84.6%); Eire has the highest ARPC at £88,421." |

> **Region classification (mutually exclusive + collectively exhaustive):**
>
> | Region | Countries | Revenue share |
> |--------|-----------|---------------|
> | UK & Ireland | United Kingdom, Eire, Channel Islands | 87.4% |
> | Europe | Austria…Switzerland (21 EU/EEA + European Community) | 10.3% |
> | Asia Pacific | Australia, Hong Kong, Japan, Singapore | 2.0% |
> | Middle East & Africa | Bahrain, Israel, Lebanon, RSA, Saudi Arabia, UAE | 0.1% |
> | Americas | Brazil, Canada, USA | 0.1% |
> | Unspecified | rows without a country code | 0.0% |
>
> Every country maps to exactly one region (see `data_model.md`), so the shares
> **sum to 100%** and regional revenue equals total revenue.
>
> **No weekday analysis here** — revenue-by-weekday lives on Page 2; this page
> instead shows country-level customer economics (customers, ARPC, % of total).

## Page 6 — Customer Retention (Cohort Analysis)

*Purpose: how long do customers stay, and how much is each cohort worth over
time. Data source: `CohortRetention` + `CohortSummary` (Phase 4).*

| Area | Visual | Fields / Measure |
|------|--------|------------------|
| KPI row (6 cards) | Card | **Total Customers** (4,339), **Repeat Customer Rate** (65.57%), **Avg 1-Month Retention** (22.7%), **Avg 6-Month Retention** (27.2%), **Best 1-Month Retention** (2010-12 · 36.6%), **Founding Cohort Revenue** (50.7% · £4,502,010 attributed) |
| Retention heatmap | Matrix | Rows = CohortRetention[cohort_month]; Columns = CohortRetention[cohort_index] (M0…M12); Values = **Retention Rate** (colour-graded navy→gold) |
| Retention decay | Line chart | X = CohortRetention[cohort_index]; Y = **Retention Rate** (gold = cohort-size weighted avg across cohorts with observed data; blue = founding 2010-12 cohort) |
| Cohort lifecycle | Table | All 13 cohorts in two compact side-by-side tables (labelled "all 13 cohorts shown"): Cohort, Customers, Repeat Rate, Lifetime Revenue, Revenue per Customer |
| Insight | Text callout | "Retention settles at 26–30% from month 3; the founding Dec 2010 cohort rebounded to 50.3% at M11 (peak season) and drives 50.7% of cohort revenue." |

> **Provenance:** all cohort numbers are computed in SQL
> (`sql/06_cohort_retention_analysis.sql`), validated against an independent
> pandas implementation (`sql/cohort_validation.py`, 12/12 PASS), and imported as
> the standalone `CohortRetention` / `CohortSummary` tables — **no relationship
> to the fact table**. M0 = **100.0%** by construction; **future months are
> blank** (never 0%) because they don't exist yet — the matrix renders them as
> "·". Heatmap cells on the page read 100.0% for every cohort's first column.
>
> **Weighted-retention definition (decay line, must not be an unweighted
> average):** at each cohort month N, `Retention Rate` =
> `SUM(active_customers) ÷ SUM(cohort_size)` computed over **only the cohorts
> with observed data at N** — future/unavailable periods are absent and are
> never treated as zero. This is the same aggregation validated against
> SQL/Pandas for M0..M12 (`validate_pbi.py`, max deviation 0.00 pp). KPI values
> and subtitles are sized to render fully at the 1280×800 canvas (no truncation).

## Interactivity spec

1. **Slicers** (top band, all pages): Date range, RFM Segment, Region, Product Category.
2. **Cross-filtering** enabled on all visuals; **cross-highlight** kept on.
3. **Tooltips**: every chart exposes Revenue/Orders/Units under the hovered slice.
4. **Page navigation**: left rail with 7 icons (Overview, Sales & Trends,
   Customer Intelligence, Product Performance, Geographic Performance,
   Customer Retention, Product Detail).
5. **Number formatting** consistent: currency `£`, counts with thousands separators,
   percentages `0.0%` (growth can be `+0.0%;-0.0%`).

## Cross-page consistency rules

- Title/subtitle placement, KPI card height, card spacing, section headers,
  fonts, currency and percentage formats are identical on every page.
- Every metric has one definition used across pages (e.g. Total Revenue is
  always £10,619,986.68; "attributed" figures are always labelled as such).
- No duplicate analysis: weekday → Page 2 only; region/country economics →
  Page 5 only; RFM segments → Pages 1 & 3 only; cohort retention → Page 6 only.
